# Electromagnetic Induction — Faraday's & Lenz's Law
## Narration script (timed to the 3:01 video, 1920×1080 @ 24 fps)

Pacing target ≈ 2.5 words/second. Timestamps are scene boundaries.

---

### 0:00 – 0:05 · Title
> Electromagnetic induction. Faraday's law, Lenz's law, and the machines they run.

### 0:05 – 0:23 · Magnetic Flux
> Everything starts with flux. Flux is B dot A — B, times the area, times the cosine
> of theta. And theta is the angle between the field and the **area vector**, the normal
> to the loop — never between the field and the plane. Watch: when the normal points
> along B, every field line threads the loop and flux is maximum. Rotate to ninety
> degrees and the field now skims past the plane — flux falls to zero. Flux is a scalar,
> measured in weber. For N turns we talk about flux linkage, N phi.

### 0:23 – 0:49 · Faraday's Law
> Now the law itself. EMF equals minus N, d-phi by d-t. Push the magnet in: the flux
> climbs, and the galvanometer kicks. Stop the magnet inside the coil — the flux is
> huge, but it is no longer *changing*, so the needle drops dead to zero. That is the
> single most examined idea in this chapter: EMF depends on the **rate** of change, not
> on the amount of flux. Pull the magnet out and the flux falls, so the EMF flips sign
> and the needle swings the other way. Now the same magnet, moved faster — same change
> in flux, less time, bigger d-phi by d-t, bigger deflection. Look at the two graphs:
> the EMF curve is just the negative slope of the flux curve.

### 0:49 – 1:11 · Lenz's Law
> So what is that minus sign doing there? That is Lenz's law. The induced current always
> flows in the direction that **opposes** the change that produced it. Bring a north pole
> in: flux increases, so the coil's near face becomes a north pole and pushes back. Pull
> it away: flux decreases, the near face turns south, and the coil pulls the magnet back.
> Either way it fights you. And that is exactly why it must — if the coil helped you, you
> would get free energy. Lenz's law is conservation of energy wearing a minus sign. To
> solve any question: find the flux, decide if it rises or falls, oppose it, then use the
> right-hand rule for the sense of the current.

### 1:11 – 1:33 · AC Generator
> First machine. Spin a coil in a magnetic field. The flux through it varies as cosine
> omega-t, so the EMF — the negative of its rate of change — comes out as N B A omega
> sine omega-t. Peak EMF is N B A omega. Notice the timing: the EMF is **zero** when the
> coil plane is perpendicular to B, where the flux is largest, and **maximum** when the
> plane is parallel to B, where the flux is zero. Flux and EMF are a quarter cycle apart.
> Slip rings carry it out, and that is every power station on earth.

### 1:33 – 1:51 · Eddy Currents
> Drop the same magnet down two pipes. Through plastic it simply falls. Through copper it
> crawls. Nothing is touching it — but the moving magnet drives closed loops of current in
> the copper wall, and by Lenz's law those eddy currents oppose the motion. You feel that
> as a braking force. We use it deliberately in induction cooktops and furnaces, in the
> electromagnetic brakes of trains, and to damp galvanometers. Where we don't want it —
> inside a transformer — we laminate the core into thin insulated sheets.

### 1:51 – 2:09 · Transformer
> Second machine, and this one is mutual induction. Alternating current in the primary
> makes an alternating flux in the iron core. That flux threads the secondary, so an EMF
> appears there: epsilon-two equals minus M, d-I-one by d-t. The voltages share the turns
> ratio — secondary over primary equals N-s over N-p — and the currents go the other way,
> because an ideal transformer changes voltage, never power. And the reason your phone
> charger needs AC: a steady direct current gives a constant flux, d-phi by d-t is zero,
> and the secondary is dead.

### 2:09 – 2:29 · Motional EMF
> You don't even need a magnet to move. Slide a conducting rod along rails in a field into
> the page. The enclosed area grows, flux is B l x, so the EMF is B l v. The free charges
> feel q v cross B, they pile up, and current flows — up the rod, round through the
> resistor. That current sits in the same field, so the rod feels a force B I l pointing
> **backwards**. Lenz again. You must keep pushing, and the power you supply, B squared l
> squared v squared over R, is exactly what heats the resistor.

### 2:29 – 2:47 · Solved Example
> Exam pattern. Two hundred turns, radius five centimetres, field dropping from zero point
> six to zero point two tesla in a tenth of a second. Area is pi r squared — convert the
> centimetres first — seven point eight five times ten to the minus three. The rate is
> zero point four over zero point one, four tesla per second. So the EMF is N A dB by dt:
> six point two eight volts. Divide by four ohms and the current is one point five seven
> amperes. And by Lenz's law it flows so as to *maintain* the dying flux.

### 2:47 – 3:01 · Formula Sheet
> Everything in one frame. Flux, Faraday, induced charge — which depends only on the total
> change in flux, never on how fast. Motional EMF, the rotating coil, the rotating rod,
> self induction and mutual induction. Learn the sign, the rate, and the opposition, and
> every question in this chapter is built out of those three ideas.
